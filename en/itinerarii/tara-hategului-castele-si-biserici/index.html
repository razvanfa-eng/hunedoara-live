Hațeg Country: castles, stone churches and Sarmizegetusa

The Geopark, Densuș church, Ulpia Traiana, the castles at Săcel and Păclișa and Mălăiești fortress, over two days around Hațeg.

2 days · 7 stops

Hațeg Country packs geology, Roman remains and noble castles into a small area. Day one follows the Hațeg–Densuș–Sarmizegetusa line; day two links the castles and the fortress at the foot of the Retezat.

Day 1 — Dinosaurs and Romans

  1. Hațeg Country UNESCO Geopark UNESCO Global Geopark · Hațeg Country

    The dwarf dinosaur geopark: over 1,000 km² of Țara Hațegului, internationally recognised since 2005 and a UNESCO Global Geopark since 2015.

  2. Densuș Church Historic monument · Hațeg Country

    One of the oldest stone churches in Romania still in use, built from reused Roman materials.

  3. Ulpia Traiana Sarmizegetusa Roman archaeological site · Hațeg Country

    The capital of Roman Dacia, founded after the conquest of 106 AD — forum, amphitheatre and temples in the village of Sarmizegetusa.

Day 2 — Castles and fortresses

  1. Nopcsa Castle, Săcel Castle · Hațeg Country

    The Nopcsa family seat, linked to the paleontologist baron Franz Nopcsa — restored and reopened to the public in 2025.

  2. Pogány Castle, Păclișa optional Castle · Totești

    A two-storey Baroque castle with 22 rooms, in a park with a stream, built around 1800 6 km from Hațeg; a children's hospital now operates here.

    Now a children's hospital: admire it from outside; visiting is not documented.

  3. Mălăiești Fortress Medieval fortress · Hațeg Country

    A 14th-century noble fortress with a keep and curtain wall, restored with EU funds, at the foot of the Retezat.

  4. Prislop Monastery optional Monastery · Silvașu de Sus, Hațeg Country

    Founded in 1564, a pilgrimage site linked to Father Arsenie Boca.
