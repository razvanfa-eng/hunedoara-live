In the footsteps of the Dacians

From the fortresses of the Orăștie Mountains to the Roman baths of Geoagiu-Băi and the capital of Roman Dacia — two days through the county's ancient history.

2 days · 7 stops

The route starts in Orăștie, the gateway to the Dacian fortresses, climbs to Sarmizegetusa Regia and on the second day moves on to the Roman period: Geoagiu-Băi, Deva and Ulpia Traiana Sarmizegetusa.

Day 1 — The Dacian fortresses

  1. Dacian Fortresses Area Historic zone · North

    The Orăștie Mountains — the fortified core of the Dacian Kingdom, UNESCO heritage since 1999.

  2. Orăștie Medieval Citadel optional Medieval fortress · Orăștie

    A 12th-century Saxon fortification, reopened to the public in 2024 after three years of restoration.

  3. Sarmizegetusa Regia UNESCO archaeological site · Orăștie Mountains

    The capital of the Dacian kingdom, on a 1,200 m summit — UNESCO World Heritage since 1999.

  4. Podul Gerosu – Fețele Albe Fortress trail optional Mountain hiking · Șureanu Mountains

    The shortest marked trail in the Dacian fortress area, to the lesser-visited Fețele Albe ruins.

    Duration: about 3 hours

Day 2 — The Roman period

  1. Germisara Roman Baths Roman archaeological site · Orăștie

    The Roman baths of Geoagiu-Băi, with pools cut into the rock, where gold offerings were made to the deities of the warm springs.

  2. Museum of Dacian and Roman Civilisation — Magna Curia Museum · Deva

    The baroque Magna Curia palace, in central Deva, houses the history collections of the Museum of Dacian and Roman Civilisation and a lapidarium with over a hundred archaeological pieces.

  3. Ulpia Traiana Sarmizegetusa Roman archaeological site · Hațeg Country

    The capital of Roman Dacia, founded after the conquest of 106 AD — forum, amphitheatre and temples in the village of Sarmizegetusa.
