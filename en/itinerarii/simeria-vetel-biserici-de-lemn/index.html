Simeria, Vețel and the wooden churches along the Mureș

The Dendrological Park, the castles at Simeria and Mintia, Micia fort and five churches and fortifications in Ilia, Șoimuș, Beriu and Dobra.

2 days · 9 stops

Beyond its larger towns, the Mureș corridor hides parks, castles and wooden churches. Day one stays around Simeria and Vețel; day two follows the Mureș towards Ilia and the surrounding villages.

Day 1 — Simeria and Vețel

  1. Simeria Arboretum Dendrological park · Simeria

    Romania's oldest and largest dendrological park, with over 2,100 tree and shrub species on about 70 hectares.

  2. Béla Fáy Manor Historic monument · Simeria

    The manor of the Gyulay, Kun, Fáy and Ocskay families, at the heart of the Simeria Arboretum.

  3. Gyulay Castle, Mintia Castle · Vețel

    The Gyulay family's castle from 1641, rebuilt in Classicist style in 1834, in a 5-hectare park by the Mureș.

  4. Micia Roman Fort optional Roman fort · Vețel

    The ruins of a large fort and a Roman settlement with a street grid, baths, amphitheatre and port, on the Mureș bank.

Day 2 — Towards Ilia

  1. Red Bastion — Gabriel Bethlen's Birthplace Historic monument · Ilia

    The 1582 bastion of Ilia's border fortress, known as the birthplace of Prince Gabriel Bethlen.

  2. Wooden Church, Bretea Mureșană Wooden church · Ilia

    A wooden church consecrated on 3 March 1653, with two inscriptions in Romanian written in Cyrillic letters.

  3. Wooden Church, Șoimuș optional Wooden church · Șoimuș

    An oak-log church built in 1705, with an iconostasis of icons painted on glass in 1830.

  4. Church of the Dormition, Beriu optional Church · Beriu

    Beriu's village church, rebuilt in 1894–1897 after a fire, with a massive tower and a Baroque-style cap.

  5. St Paraskeva Church, Lăpușnic optional Historic monument · Dobra

    A 17th-century stone church with a vault painted in 1726 on a lining of wooden boards.
